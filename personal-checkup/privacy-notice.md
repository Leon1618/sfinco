# Personal Digital Check-up — Client Data Handling Notice

Plain-English notice for check-up clients, alongside `smb-audits/privacy-notice.md` for business clients. Same underlying reasoning, adjusted for what's actually different when the client is a person rather than a business.

## Where Sfinco likely stands under the Privacy Act

Same as the SMB audit: as a sole trader under $3 million turnover, Sfinco most likely falls under the **Privacy Act 1988**'s small business operator exemption — and that exemption is about *Sfinco's* size as the entity handling the information, not about whether the person whose data it is happens to be a business or an individual. So, correcting an overstatement in an earlier draft of `methodology.md`: the exemption doesn't stop applying just because this product serves individuals rather than businesses.

What's genuinely different here isn't the legal exemption — it's who's in the room. On a Family Digital Safety Check, the person paying (an adult child) and the person whose phone gets looked at (their parent) aren't the same person. That's a real thing worth being careful about regardless of what the exemption technically allows, which is why `family-framing.md`'s hard rule exists: the parent is always part of the actual review, never reviewed about. Consent from the person the check-up is actually about matters here more than any exemption does.

## What gets collected

- Contact details given at booking (name, email, phone).
- What's observed during the check-up conversation — device type, general settings, what's already in good shape.
- Notes taken to write the one-page summary.

## What doesn't get collected

No passwords, no PINs, no banking logins, no photos or messages read beyond what's needed to confirm a setting (e.g. confirming Face ID is on, not looking through photos). Nothing is ever asked for or typed into anything Sfinco controls.

## Where it's kept, and for how long

Same as the SMB audit: kept on Leo's own device, not uploaded anywhere beyond the email used to send the summary. Kept for 12 months in case of a follow-up, then deleted — sooner on request.

## On the Family Digital Safety Check specifically

The information belongs to the person whose phone it is, not the person who paid for it. If a parent ever wants to see what's held about them, correct it, or have it deleted, that request is honoured directly from them — it doesn't need to go through whoever booked the check-up.
