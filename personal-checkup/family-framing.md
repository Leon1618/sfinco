# Family Digital Safety Check — what actually changes

Internal note. Same product as the Personal Digital Check-up (`methodology.md`) — same three stages, same six areas, same price. This doc is only about what's different when the buyer is an adult child, not the person whose phone gets looked at.

## The one real difference: who books it, and how the first conversation goes

The free 20-minute conversation (Stage 1) usually happens with the adult child first, not the parent — because they're the one who searched for this and is paying. But the review itself (Stage 2) is never done *about* the parent without them there. That's a hard line, not a nice-to-have:

- The first call with the adult child is about logistics and context: what's prompted this, what device the parent uses, any specific worry (a recent scam call, a locked-out account) worth knowing about going in.
- Before Stage 2 is booked, the parent needs to actually agree to it and be part of the call. If the adult child wants to "just get it done" without involving their parent directly, that's not something Sfinco does — say so plainly, the same way `smb-audits/methodology.md` says no to scope that doesn't fit.
- The written summary (Stage 3) goes to whoever's paying, but the conversation happens with the parent in the room, hearing it the same way they'd hear it if they'd booked it themselves.

## Marketing angle — what changes in the copy, not the product

The buying trigger is different, so the words that get someone to book are different, even though what they're booking is identical:

- **Personal Digital Check-up** copy speaks to someone booking for themselves — plain, direct, "get a clear picture of your own phone and accounts."
- **Family Digital Safety Check** copy speaks to the adult child's actual worry — not "is my parent confused," but "I'd feel better knowing the basics are covered, and I don't want to be the one lecturing them about it." That's a real, specific relief this sells: someone else has the conversation, so it isn't a family argument.

Neither page should ever describe the parent as behind, confused, or a target because of their age. The scam-vulnerability reality is genuine and worth naming once, plainly — but the person being checked up on is never spoken about as if they're not capable. If a sentence would embarrass someone to have read back to them, it doesn't go on the page.
