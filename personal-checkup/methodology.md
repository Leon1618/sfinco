# Personal Digital Check-up — Methodology

Internal working document. Same shape as `smb-audits/methodology.md`, adapted for an individual rather than a business — a plain-English review of one person's phone, accounts, and scam awareness, not a company's systems.

Sold two ways from the same product: **Personal Digital Check-up** (bought by the person themselves) and **Family Digital Safety Check** (bought by an adult child, for a parent). Delivery is identical either way — see `family-framing.md` for what actually changes between the two.

## Principles

- **Dignity first.** This is not "help for someone who can't keep up." Nobody is condescended to, on the call, in the report, or in a single line of marketing copy — including when the buyer is an adult child talking about a parent who isn't in the room. If a sentence could read as talking down to someone, rewrite it.
- **Plain English throughout.** Same rule as the SMB audit — explain the technical term the first time it comes up, in a bracket, then never again.
- **No scare tactics.** The goal is confidence, not fear. Sfinco's own brand thesis is "protecting people who don't know they need protecting" — protecting, not frightening.
- **One conversation, not a lecture.** This works best as a relaxed chat about how someone actually uses their phone, not a quiz they can fail.
- **Built on what already exists.** Domains below follow the same shape as Sfinco Guides Vol 1 (iPhone Protection) — this check-up is that book's content, delivered as a conversation instead of a chapter, for someone who'd rather talk it through than read it.

## The three stages

### 1. A relaxed conversation (free, ~20 min, phone or video)

Same spirit as the SMB audit's intake, adjusted for a much lighter subject: what devices someone uses day to day, what they're already a bit unsure about, and whether anything's actually happened (a suspicious call, a strange text, a locked-out account) that prompted looking into this now.

If booked as a Family Digital Safety Check, this conversation is usually with the adult child first — see `family-framing.md` for how that changes the flow, then still involves the parent directly for the review itself. This is never done *about* someone without them being part of it.

Output: a sense of what to focus on, and a fixed price confirmed before anything else happens.

### 2. The review (30–45 min, done together, on a call)

Walked through live, on the phone or a video call, looking at the actual phone or accounts together — not a form to fill in alone. See **What gets checked** below.

### 3. A short written summary

Nothing like the SMB audit's multi-page report — this is a one-page, plain-English note: two or three things worth doing, in order, and a quick note on what's already being done well. Sent by email, and talked through on the call itself so nothing lands cold in an inbox.

## What gets checked

Six areas, following Sfinco Guides Vol 1's own chapter structure — this check-up *is* that book's content, just delivered as a conversation:

- **Locking the front door** — passcode or Face ID/Touch ID set up, auto-lock timing, Stolen Device Protection (or the Android/Windows equivalent) turned on.
- **Apple ID / Google account security** — the account that everything else depends on: MFA on, recovery details current, nobody else has access who shouldn't.
- **App permissions** — a quick look at what apps can see location, contacts, or photos, and whether that still makes sense.
- **Scams and phishing instincts** — not a test, a conversation: what a suspicious call or text actually looks like, and the one habit that matters most — pause, and check with someone before acting on anything urgent.
- **Wi-Fi and being out and about** — safe habits on public Wi-Fi, without needing to understand what a VPN is to be safe.
- **Find My / theft response** — turned on, and a shared understanding of what to do if the phone is ever lost.

Banking and payment apps come up naturally inside a couple of these (locking the front door, scam instincts) rather than as their own checklist item — the point is making sure money-related apps get the same basic protections as everything else, not turning this into anything resembling financial advice.

## What this is not

- Not financial advice, and never framed as such — even with Leo's banking and RG146/RG206 background genuinely relevant to spotting scam patterns, nothing here recommends a financial product or strategy. Stay inside device and account safety.
- Not a substitute for reporting an actual scam or fraud in progress — if something urgent comes up during a conversation, the advice is to contact the bank or Scamwatch directly and immediately, not to wait for a scheduled check-up.
- Not ongoing monitoring — a point-in-time conversation, the same honest framing as the SMB audit.

## Regulatory flag

Handling an individual's personal information (not a business's) means the **Privacy Act 1988** applies more directly here than it does for Sfinco's business clients under the small-business exemption — see `smb-audits/privacy-notice.md` for the reasoning on that exemption, which does not extend to how individuals' own personal data gets handled. A short, plain-English privacy note for this product specifically is still needed before this goes live — flagged in `README.md` as outstanding.
