If something feels wrong

Scams & fraud

Where to check a scam, report one, or get help if your details have already been used.

Scamwatch

Run by the ACCC. Report a scam and see what's currently trending across Australia.

Visit scamwatch.gov.au →

IDCARE

Australia's free national identity and cyber support service, if your details have been compromised.

Visit idcare.org →

Australian Cyber Security Hotline

The government's main number for cybercrime and online scams, any time.

Call 1300 292 371 →

Have I Been Pwned

Check whether your email address has turned up in a known data breach.

Visit haveibeenpwned.com →

Targeting Scams report

The National Anti-Scam Centre's own annual data on what's actually costing Australians money, and who's being targeted.

Visit scamwatch.gov.au →

Keep Scam SAFE

National Seniors Australia's ongoing campaign and simple framework for pausing before acting on anything unexpected.

Visit nationalseniors.com.au →

ReportCyber

The government's official portal for reporting a cybercrime, hack, or security vulnerability, separate from reporting a scam through Scamwatch.

Visit cyber.gov.au/report →

ACSC Alert Service

Free email alerts from the Australian Cyber Security Centre when a significant new threat or scam is doing the rounds.

Visit cyber.gov.au →
The basics that matter most

Passwords & accounts

The single biggest upgrade most people can make is using a different password everywhere. These make that realistic.

Bitwarden

A password manager with a genuinely free plan, so you can stop reusing the same password everywhere.

Visit bitwarden.com →

Your phone's built-in password manager

Google Password Manager on Android, or Passwords (iCloud Keychain) on iPhone. Already on your phone, free, and does the basics well.

Find your device below →

Google Authenticator / Microsoft Authenticator

Free apps that add a second step to your logins, so a stolen password alone isn't enough to get in. Search for either by name in your phone's app store.

For the whole family

Family & seniors

Resources built for exactly the audience Sfinco cares most about, and for parents keeping an eye on younger family members too.

eSafety Commissioner

Australia's online safety regulator, with guidance written specifically for older Australians as well as young people.

Visit esafety.gov.au →

Moneysmart

ASIC's plain-English guide to money and scam safety, no financial jargon required.

Visit moneysmart.gov.au →
For business owners

Small business

Free government guidance that pairs naturally with a Sfinco SMB Cyber Audit.

ACSC Essential Eight

The government's baseline checklist for protecting a small business, written for owners, not IT teams.

Visit cyber.gov.au →

Notifiable Data Breaches scheme

What you're legally required to do if customer data is exposed, from the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

Visit oaic.gov.au →
Straight from the source

Your devices

Official security settings from the people who actually built your device. The Sfinco Safety Guides walk through these step by step, but the official pages are free right now.

iPhone & Mac

Apple's official support site covers passcodes, Face ID, Apple ID, and iCloud security.

Visit support.apple.com →

Windows

Microsoft's official support site covers Windows Hello, Windows Defender, and account security.

Visit support.microsoft.com →

Android

Google's official support site covers your Google account, app permissions, and Play Protect.

Visit support.google.com →
Protecting your money

Money & banking

Not financial advice, just where to look and who to call.

Moneysmart

ASIC's guide to spotting investment scams and protecting your super, in plain English.

Visit moneysmart.gov.au →

Your bank's official fraud line

Save it in your phone under a name you'll recognise, using the number on the back of your card, never a number from a text message.

Not something to act on

Just for interest

This one isn't a safety tool, it's just a genuinely interesting thing to look at. The numbers move fast and look dramatic, but that's mostly automated scanning traffic bouncing around the internet, not a sign anything's wrong with your own devices.

Fortinet Threat Map

A live, moving map of cyberattack traffic being detected around the world, from Fortinet's FortiGuard Labs. Purely for curiosity, nothing here requires you to do anything.

Visit fortiguard.fortinet.com →
