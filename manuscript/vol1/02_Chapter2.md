# Locking the front door


Passcode, Face ID, and what to do before your phone leaves your hands

Think of your iPhone as a house. Your contacts, your bank apps, your photos, your emails — they are all inside, and they are all valuable. The front door to that house is your passcode and Face ID. If someone can get past that door, they have access to everything.

This chapter covers the three layers of your iPhone's front-door security — and one feature that most people have never heard of, which can make an enormous difference if your phone is ever stolen.

None of this takes more than ten minutes to set up. Let's go through it step by step.


## Layer 1 — Your passcode


Every iPhone has a passcode — a number or combination you enter to unlock your phone. But not all passcodes are equally strong, and the default settings are not always the safest.

What most people use — and why it's not enough

The most common iPhone passcodes in Australia are: 123456, 000000, 111111, your birth year, and your birth date. Scammers and thieves know this. When someone steals a phone and wants to get in, these are the first things they try.

A four-digit PIN gives 10,000 possible combinations. A six-digit PIN gives 1,000,000. A custom alphanumeric code — using letters, numbers, and symbols — gives billions. The difference matters.

> **ℹ DID YOU KNOW?**
>
> Research by the security firm ESET found that approximately one in five people use one of the top 10 most predictable passcodes. If you are using your birth year, your house number, or a repeating digit, you are not as protected as you think.



How to change your passcode right now

Settings path:  Settings  >  Face ID & Passcode  >  Change Passcode

Open Settings  on your iPhone home screen — it looks like a grey box with gears.

Scroll down and tap  Face ID & Passcode.

You will be prompted to enter your current passcode.

Tap Change Passcode.  Enter your current passcode again when prompted.

Tap Passcode Options  at the top of the screen. You will see four choices.

Select Custom Alphanumeric Code  for the strongest option — or Custom Numeric Code for a longer number if you prefer.

Enter your new passcode twice  to confirm it.

*[ SCREENSHOT: Settings > Face ID & Passcode screen showing Change Passcode option ]*



The Face ID & Passcode screen in Settings. Tap 'Change Passcode' and then 'Passcode Options' to choose a stronger format.

*[ SCREENSHOT: Passcode Options screen showing Custom Alphanumeric Code selected ]*



Tap 'Passcode Options' to reveal the full list. 'Custom Alphanumeric Code' is the strongest choice — it allows letters, numbers, and symbols.

> **★ TIP**
>
> Choose a passphrase rather than a random string of characters. Something like 'Noosa2019beach!' is far easier to remember than 'xK#92mP!' and just as strong. A phrase that means something to you but nothing to anyone else is ideal.



> **⚠ WARNING**
>
> Do not use: your date of birth, your address, your phone number, your child's name, your pet's name, or any number or word that someone who knows you could guess. Also avoid: 123456, 000000, 111111, and your birth year — these are the first things a thief will try.




## Layer 2 — Face ID


Face ID uses the camera on the front of your iPhone to recognise your face and unlock the phone — without you needing to type your passcode every time. It is fast, convenient, and surprisingly secure.

Apple's Face ID system maps over 30,000 invisible dots across your face to create a mathematical model unique to you. It cannot be fooled by a photograph. It updates itself gradually over time to account for changes in your appearance — glasses, a beard, ageing.

If Face ID is not yet set up on your phone, or if it has been behaving unreliably, here is how to set it up properly.

Setting up Face ID

Settings path:  Settings  >  Face ID & Passcode  >  Set Up Face ID

Open Settings  and tap Face ID & Passcode.

Tap Set Up Face ID.

Hold your iPhone  in portrait orientation at eye level, about 25–30 cm from your face — roughly arm's length.

Follow the on-screen instructions:  move your head slowly in a circle so the camera can capture your face from different angles. You will do this twice.

Tap Done  when complete. Face ID is now active.

*[ SCREENSHOT: Face ID setup screen showing circular face scan animation ]*



The Face ID setup screen. Follow the circle with your head to let the camera map your face from all angles. The process takes about 30 seconds.

> **★ TIP**
>
> If you wear glasses, set up Face ID both with and without them. Go to Settings > Face ID & Passcode > Set Up an Alternate Appearance to add a second scan. This also works for significant changes in appearance such as a new beard or different hairstyle.



What Face ID is used for

Once set up, Face ID can be used to:

Unlock your iPhone

Authorise Apple Pay purchases

Confirm App Store and iTunes purchases

AutoFill saved passwords in Safari

Unlock third-party apps that support Face ID — including most banking apps

> **✓ WELL DONE IF YOU HAVE THIS**
>
> If your banking app uses Face ID to log in, that is a good sign — it means someone who picks up your phone cannot open your banking app even if they somehow know your iPhone passcode, because your face is the second lock.




## Layer 3 — Auto-lock


Auto-lock controls how quickly your iPhone locks itself after you stop using it. When your phone locks, the screen turns off and Face ID or your passcode is required to get back in.

Many people set this to a long time — or even Never — because it feels convenient. But a phone that stays unlocked is a phone that anyone can pick up and use.

Settings path:  Settings  >  Display & Brightness  >  Auto-Lock

The recommended setting is 30 seconds or 1 minute. This might feel short at first, but you will quickly get used to it — and it means your phone locks itself almost immediately if you put it down, drop it, or have it taken from you.

*[ SCREENSHOT: Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock set to 30 Seconds ]*



The Auto-Lock setting under Display & Brightness. 30 seconds is the recommended setting — it locks the phone quickly without being disruptive during normal use.

> **⚠ WARNING**
>
> If your Auto-Lock is set to 'Never', your phone will stay fully accessible for as long as the screen is on — even if you leave it on a table in a café. Change this to 30 seconds or 1 minute today.




## The feature most people have never heard of — Stolen Device Protection


In 2024, Apple introduced a feature called Stolen Device Protection. It was created in response to a specific and alarming pattern of theft: thieves watching people type their passcodes in public — at cafés, on public transport, in shopping centres — then stealing the phone and using the passcode to completely take over the victim's Apple account.

With the old system, someone who knew your passcode could:

Change your Apple ID password

Turn off Find My iPhone — making the phone untraceable

Remove Face ID and add their own

Add a new recovery key — locking you out of your own account permanently

Access and drain your banking apps

Stolen Device Protection changes this. When it is turned on and your phone is away from your trusted locations (home and work), certain sensitive actions require Face ID — and cannot be overridden by your passcode. This means that even if a thief knows your passcode, they cannot take over your Apple account without your face.

How to turn on Stolen Device Protection

Settings path:  Settings  >  Face ID & Passcode  >  Stolen Device Protection

Open Settings  and tap Face ID & Passcode.

Enter your passcode  when prompted.

Scroll down to Stolen Device Protection  and tap Turn On Protection.

Confirm  by authenticating with Face ID.

*[ SCREENSHOT: Settings > Face ID & Passcode > Stolen Device Protection toggle — turned ON ]*



The Stolen Device Protection toggle in Settings. This is one of the most important security features Apple has ever introduced — turn it on now if you haven't already.

> **ℹ DID YOU KNOW?**
>
> Stolen Device Protection is available on iPhones running iOS 17.3 or later. To check your iOS version: Settings > General > About > Software Version. If your software is older than iOS 17.3, update it first (Settings > General > Software Update) and then enable this feature.



> **★ TIP**
>
> Make sure your home address and work address are saved in your iPhone's Significant Locations so Stolen Device Protection recognises them as trusted places. Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > System Services > Significant Locations. This prevents the feature from triggering inconveniently at familiar locations.
