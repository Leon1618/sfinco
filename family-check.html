Who it's for

You're not wrong to be a little worried

Scam calls and texts target older Australians more than most, and it's a genuine, reasonable thing to want your parent's basics covered. This isn't about them being behind or confused — most people, of any age, have never actually gone through their phone's settings on purpose. It's just never come up.

How it works

The check-up process

  • A first conversation with you

    About 20 minutes, free, usually with you rather than your parent — what's prompted this, what device they use, anything specific worth knowing going in.

  • The check-up, done with your parent

    30–45 minutes, on a call, with your parent — never done about them without them there. Locking the front door, their main account's security, app permissions, spotting a scam, safe Wi-Fi habits, and what to do if the phone's ever lost.

  • A one-page summary

    Two or three things worth doing, in plain English, and what's already going well. Sent to whoever's paying, talked through with your parent on the call itself.

The one rule we don't bend on

Your parent is always part of the actual check-up — never reviewed without them, never talked about as if they're not in the room. You can set it up, but the conversation happens with them, not around them.
