# Sfinco — Brand and Voice Guidelines (reference)

## Voice

- Warm, human, conversational — never corporate or cold.
- Plain English always — no jargon without an immediate plain-English explanation.
- Humble and honest — no overclaiming, no misrepresenting credentials or experience.
- Short and punchy for headings and calls to action; fuller prose for explanations.
- Australian — references, examples, and pricing in AUD and Australian context.
- Sunshine Coast local — ground examples in Noosa, Tewantin, Maroochydore, Eumundi, Cooroy where it fits naturally. Don't force it into every sentence.

## Audience

**Primary:** Australians aged 60–75 on the Sunshine Coast. Digitally exposed, financially comfortable, trust very few people. Do not want to be lectured or patronised. Want to feel confident, not scared. Write copy that could be read aloud to a 70-year-old without confusing them — simplify before Leo has to ask.

**Secondary:** Small business owners in Noosa and surrounds — cafes, clinics, real estate, tradies.

## What to avoid

- AI-sounding phrases: "genuinely", "it's worth noting that", "absolutely", "certainly", "straightforward".
- Bullet-point overload — prose first, lists only when genuinely clearer.
- Tech jargon without explanation.
- Fear-based messaging — the site should build empowerment and confidence, not alarm. Real scam examples are fine and useful, but always pair the scare with the fix.
- Overclaiming on regulatory matters — always flag what requires professional advice, and never imply SfincoAssist Phase 2 (live Open Banking data, financial advice) is available before it exists.

## Colours (established for the Sfinco Guides, reusable for the site)

- Blue `#1B4F8A` — primary brand colour, headings and accents.
- Amber `#C47F00` — secondary brand colour, subtitles and highlight elements.

These are the only brand colours currently locked in. If the site needs a broader palette (backgrounds, states, etc.), derive tints/shades from these two rather than introducing unrelated colours, and check with Leo before finalising.

## Design motifs already established (from the Guides series)

- Sphinx motif — used on book covers, appropriate for a site logomark or favicon treatment.
- Callout-box system used throughout the books: Tip (amber), Warning (red), Did You Know (blue), Well Done (green), Critical (purple), Real Story (amber), Scam Example (dark). These map naturally to on-site "tip" or "did you know" callouts if the site includes any educational content blocks.

## Tone check

Before publishing any page copy, it should pass this test: would this feel like a trustworthy, warm local person explaining something to a friend's parent — or does it read like a corporate SaaS landing page? If it's the latter, rewrite it.
