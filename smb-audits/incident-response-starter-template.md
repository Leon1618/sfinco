# The Sfinco Emergency Plan — one-page starter

Delivered with every audit alongside the report. Duplicate this file per client, fill in the brackets, delete this line and the two below before sending. This is deliberately not the SANS/NIST-style incident response plan a larger organisation would build (preparation, identification, containment, eradication, recovery, lessons learned) — it's a single page a busy owner will actually read and use, covering the same ground in plain English. If a client later wants the fuller version, that's retainer territory, not this document.

Fits on one page. Print it, or keep it somewhere everyone on the team can find it without asking Leo or IT first.

---

# If something goes wrong — [Business Name]'s emergency plan

Prepared by Leo Tavares, Sfinco · [Date]

## Call these people first, in this order

1. **[Bank's fraud line]** — if money has moved or a payment looks wrong. Use the number on the back of your card, never one from an email or text.
2. **[IT/Sfinco contact]** — Leo, [phone], [email] — to help work out what happened and what to do next.
3. **[Relevant platform support]** — [e.g. Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, your POS provider] if an account or system has been locked out or compromised.

## The first thirty minutes

- **If an account looks compromised:** change that password immediately, from a different device if possible, and turn on MFA if it isn't already on.
- **If a payment or transfer looks wrong:** call the bank's fraud line straight away (see above) — the faster this happens, the better the chance of stopping or reversing it.
- **If a device is lost or stolen:** report it to police, and if it holds business data, change the passwords for anything that was logged in on it.
- **If you're not sure what's happened, just that something feels wrong:** stop using the affected system, and call Leo. A ten-minute conversation now is cheaper than guessing wrong for a week.

## If customer data may have been exposed

Businesses that handle personal information may have obligations under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme to tell the people affected, and sometimes the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC). This isn't legal advice, and Sfinco isn't a law firm, call Leo first and he'll help you work out whether this applies and what a next step looks like.

## Report it

- **Cybercrime:** ReportCyber, cyber.gov.au/report
- **Scams:** Scamwatch, scamwatch.gov.au
- **Identity theft support:** IDCARE, 1800 595 160
