Your bank will never ask for your PIN, except it's more complicated than that

The advice is true and worth remembering, but scammers have adapted around it. Here's the nuance worth knowing.

T
Tom Whitfield 24 June 2026 · 3 min read

"Your bank will never ask for your PIN or full password" is genuinely good advice, and it's still true. But scammers have adjusted their scripts to work around it, which means the advice alone isn't quite enough anymore.

What's changed

Rather than asking for your PIN outright, a scam call might ask you to "confirm" a few digits, or talk you through moving money "to a safe account" while sounding exactly like a bank fraud team. No PIN is ever spoken, but the outcome is the same.

A convincing script doesn't need your PIN if it can talk you into doing the transfer yourself.

The safer habit

Any call about suspicious activity on your account, however calm and official it sounds, is worth ending and calling back using the number on the back of your card. A genuine bank will never be offended by this, a scammer will often try to stop you doing it.

Watch for urgency dressed as helpfulness

Scam calls in this style often sound helpful and reassuring rather than threatening, which is precisely what makes them effective. Calm still means verify.
