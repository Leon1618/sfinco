Privacy and confidentiality aren't the same thing

They get used interchangeably, but they mean genuinely different things, and the difference matters.

T
Tom Whitfield 8 April 2026 · 3 min read

These two words get used as if they mean the same thing, and they don't. Knowing the difference actually changes what you should be checking for.

Privacy is about you

Privacy is your own ability to control who sees information about you and what happens to it. It's the setting on your social media account, the choice not to hand over your date of birth to a random website, the right to be left alone.

Confidentiality is a promise someone else made

Confidentiality is an agreement, spoken or written, that someone else won't share information you've given them. When your bank says your details are confidential, that's a commitment on their end, not a setting you control.

Privacy is what you control. Confidentiality is what someone else has promised.

Why the difference matters

If a business breaks confidentiality, that's a promise broken, and often a legal obligation broken too. If you give away your own information freely, nobody's broken a promise, you've simply made a privacy choice, possibly without meaning to. Knowing which one you're dealing with helps you know who's actually responsible when something goes wrong.

Worth a proper read

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner has a clear, plain-English explanation of privacy as a right. Read it at oaic.gov.au.
