Why we changed our own homepage headline

The original line was "protecting people who don't know they need protecting." It didn't survive first contact with actual feedback.

S
Sfinco 29 July 2026 · 3 min read

Early on, Sfinco's homepage led with a line I liked the sound of: protecting people who don't know they need protecting. It captured the idea behind the whole business neatly, or so I thought.

The problem with it

Read it back slowly and it says something slightly different to what I meant. It implies the reader doesn't know something obvious, which is exactly the tone this brand is built to avoid. The people Sfinco is for aren't unaware, they're just busy, and scams are specifically designed to catch busy, careful people off guard, not careless ones.

Nobody wants to be told they didn't know they needed help. Everybody wants confidence.

What replaced it

The homepage now leads with "Confidence, not confusion," which says what I actually meant without the condescending undertone. The old idea didn't disappear, it just moved into the supporting copy as "looking after the everyday things that are easy to lose track of," which credits the reader with being switched on rather than suggesting otherwise.

Why it's worth writing about

A one-line change might seem small, but for a brand built entirely on trust, tone is most of the product. If a sentence on the homepage doesn't sound like something a trustworthy local person would actually say, it gets rewritten, however much work went into it originally.
