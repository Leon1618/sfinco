That free cafe Wi-Fi might not be what it looks like

Two networks with the same name can sit in the same room, and only one of them is the cafe's.

J
Jess Carter 29 April 2026 · 3 min read

It's a genuinely simple trick. Someone sets up a Wi-Fi network with the same name as the cafe's real one, sometimes with a laptop and nothing more exotic than that, and waits for phones and laptops to connect automatically. Security researchers call it an "evil twin" network.

Why it's so easy to fall for

Most devices are set to rejoin networks they've used before without asking, which is convenient right up until a fake network borrows a familiar name. Once connected, whoever set it up can potentially see the traffic passing through, including anything not otherwise encrypted.

The network doesn't have to look suspicious. It just has to have the right name.

What actually helps

Confirm the exact network name with staff rather than picking the first one that looks right, especially if you see two similar names. Avoid logging into anything sensitive, banking especially, on public Wi-Fi at all. A VPN adds a genuine layer of protection here specifically, since it encrypts the connection regardless of which network you've landed on.
