What a VPN actually does, and doesn't

It's one of the most misunderstood tools in everyday cyber safety, oversold by some, dismissed by others.

T
Tom Whitfield 22 April 2026 · 3 min read

A VPN, virtual private network, gets talked about like it's a magic cloak of invisibility online. It isn't. It's a genuinely useful tool for a specific job, and understanding that job means you'll actually use it when it matters, rather than everywhere or nowhere.

What it actually does

A VPN encrypts the connection between your device and the internet, so anyone else on the same network, say, public Wi-Fi at a cafe or an airport, can't easily see what you're sending. That's it. That's the job.

A VPN protects the trip your data takes. It doesn't protect you once your data arrives.

What it doesn't do

A VPN doesn't stop a scam text from being convincing. It doesn't stop you from typing your password into a fake website. It doesn't make a weak password strong. It's protecting the journey your information takes across the network, not the decisions you make once you're logged in somewhere.

When it's actually worth using

Public Wi-Fi is the clearest case, a cafe, an airport, a hotel, anywhere you don't control the network. At home, on your own internet connection, a VPN matters much less. It's a specific tool for a specific situation, not something that needs to run every minute of every day.
